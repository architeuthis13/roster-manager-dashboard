1. Context & Background
Disability Support and Aged Care organisations deliver personalised, ongoing support services to vulnerable people — referred to as care recipients. These services are delivered by support workers rostered to specific shifts.
A Roster Manager ensures every care recipient has the right support worker showing up, on time, with the right qualifications, every single shift. The stakes are high: a missed shift or an unqualified worker is a safety and compliance risk for a vulnerable person.
Roster managers already have a rostering tool to allocate shifts. What they lack is operational visibility. This dashboard is not a rostering tool — it sits alongside the existing system and surfaces what needs attention right now, and what will become a problem soon.

2. Problem Statement
Without a dedicated oversight tool, roster managers are forced to:

Manually check multiple screens or reports to find problems
React to crises rather than prevent them
Manually match staff to open shifts — checking qualifications, travel proximity, hours, and care recipient requirements one by one
Accidentally underutilise permanent staff while over-relying on casuals, creating legal and cost exposure
Miss overtime obligations by approving shifts without visibility of pending leave or cumulative hours
Chase down missing compliance documents with no centralised view
Manage leave and availability requests from a separate inbox

Problems go unnoticed until they become urgent — or until a care recipient is left without support.

3. Goal
Build a command centre dashboard that gives a roster manager immediate visibility into everything that needs their attention right now, and flags problems before they become crises.
This is not a rostering tool. It does not create or edit shifts. It provides oversight and decision support on top of an existing rostering system.

4. Users
Primary — The Roster Manager
Coordinates staffing for a caseload of 20–100+ care recipients. Manages support workers with varying qualifications, availability, employment types, and working hour limits. Operates under NDIS, aged care quality standards, and the SCHADS Award. Spends most of their day firefighting — this tool should reduce that.
Out of scope: Support workers, care recipients, finance/payroll, family/guardians.

7.2 Open Shift Management — Two Modes
Shifts exist in three visually distinct states:
Shift StateLabelManager ActionNo worker assigned, not published"Needs Filling"Mode A — manager finds someonePublished, requests received"X Requests" badgeMode B — manager reviews requestsPublished, no requests yet"Published – Awaiting Requests"Monitor; auto-escalates at threshold
Mode A — Manager Finds Someone ("Find Me Someone")
Manager opens an unassigned shift. Dashboard surfaces a ranked candidate list from the eligible staff pool. Each candidate shows:

Employment type (Permanent FT / Permanent PT / Casual)
Hours this week vs contracted hours — displayed as a range when pending items exist (e.g. "22 / 25 hrs · ↑ up to 25 if pending request approved")
Overtime warning level (progressive — see Section 8)
Pending leave flag if applicable ("⚠ Pending leave overlaps this shift — assign with caution")
Compliance status — documents held, missing, or expiring — validated against shift start date
Suitability match to the care recipient
Geographic proximity — worker home suburb → shift suburb, with proximity band label (🟢 Nearby / 🟡 Moderate travel / 🔴 Significant travel)
Broken shift flag if this shift would create two periods of work with a gap on the same day

Candidate ranking defaults to:

Permanent FT — within contracted hours, compliant, suitable
Permanent PT — within contracted hours, compliant, suitable
Permanent FT/PT — overtime flagged
Casual — compliant and suitable
Casual — with warnings

Unsuitable candidates (on approved leave, missing mandatory compliance, breaching overtime) are visually demoted and flagged — never hidden.
Mode B — Manager Reviews Incoming Requests
Manager opens a shift with staff requests. Dashboard shows:

A ranked review queue of requestors, each showing the same suitability signals as Mode A
How many requests have come in and when
A "Best available (not yet requested)" suggestion — top candidate from the full staff pool who hasn't self-selected, so the manager isn't limited to whoever volunteered
Approving one request auto-declines all others
"Decline All & Find Someone" converts the shift to Mode A and pre-populates the candidate list with the best available match

Auto-escalation rule: Published shifts with no requests that cross a configurable urgency threshold (default: 4 hours before shift start) automatically escalate to "Needs Filling Now" — treated as Mode A with a visible countdown and surfaced in the urgent alerts panel.

7.3 Check-in Alert Feed
Live list of shifts that are:

Not yet checked in (with time overdue shown)
Checked in late
Missing a check-out

Sorted by severity. Each item shows: care recipient name, assigned worker name, shift time, and how overdue the action is.

7.4 Compliance Warning Flags
Inline warnings on any shift allocation where:

A required compliance document is missing or expired — always validated against the shift start date, not today's date
Worker attributes don't match the care recipient's support needs
Worker would exceed contracted hours (progressive threshold warnings)
A pending leave request overlaps the shift date
The shift is under 2 hours (SCHADS minimum engagement)
The shift creates a broken shift for the worker that day

Compliance is re-checked continuously — whenever a document is updated, a care recipient's profile changes, or a worker's leave status changes. Approved allocations are never treated as permanently clean.

7.5 Compliance Document Tracker
A list of all staff with missing, expired, or soon-to-expire documents. Each row shows: worker name, employment type, document type, expiry date or "Missing", and days overdue or days until expiry. Sorted by urgency.

7.6 Shift Timeline View (Read-Only)
A 7-day grid of all allocated shifts — for scanning, not editing. Colour-coded by alert severity. Warning badges overlaid on shifts with compliance mismatches, overtime risk, or check-in issues.

10. Geographic Proximity
No location API calls. No calculated travel times. Suburb-to-suburb display only.
Roster managers know their local geography intuitively. "Chermside → Aspley" means more to them than an API-calculated "14 minutes" they cannot verify.
Display format on candidate cards:

📍 Chermside → Aspley 🟢 Nearby

Three proximity bands (hardcoded in mock data):
BandLabelBasisSame or adjacent suburb🟢 NearbySuburb names match or are in the same postcode groupSame broad region🟡 Moderate travelPostcodes within a defined numeric rangeDifferent region🔴 Significant travelPostcodes far apart
Proximity band is a soft hint only — the manager makes the final call. Bands are pre-assigned in mock data. No calculation required.
